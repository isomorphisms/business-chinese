# Chinese: business and workplace

Goal: learn Chinese that is immediately useful for the user's own business,
workplace, and public-interaction situations rather than following a generic
tourist course.

## Current corpus shape

- `words/`: deliberately simple English headword files with one viable Chinese
  candidate per line. For now the candidates are forced into a single
  context-free order even though that representation is known to be wrong.
- `word contrasts/`: comparisons that arose from real communicative intentions
  and where the alternatives change meaning, register, speech act, implied
  relationship, commitment, or some other listener-relevant feature.
- Keep phrase-sized entries when the phrase is the real translation unit. Do not
  pretend every sentence decomposes into independent one-word substitutions.

The flat order in `words/` is a useful temporary representation, not the desired
long-term model. The long-term model is context-dependent and may require coupled
changes elsewhere in a phrase when one choice changes.

## Drill shape

- Default sessions: about 5–10 minutes.
- Words and refreshers are first-class.
- Keep pinyin, characters, English meaning, and pronunciation notes available to
  the learner as those layers are added.
- Add material from real conversations as it arises.
- Prefer communicative distinctions that have actually mattered over arbitrary
  dictionary pairings.

## Initial domains

- asking permission and making polite requests;
- posting, handing over, or showing something;
- introducing a project or business idea;
- cold-call and pitch openings;
- asking what someone thinks;
- negotiating next steps, timing, money, and responsibilities;
- ordinary workplace small talk.

Seed phrase already used in practice:

- 请问我可以把这个贴在这里吗？
  - asking politely whether something may be posted here.

Do not pad the corpus merely to make a long course. The point is useful recall.
